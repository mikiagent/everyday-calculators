How loan payments are calculated

A fixed-rate loan is repaid in equal instalments. Each payment covers that month's interest on the remaining balance, and the rest reduces the balance. Early on most of the payment is interest; later most of it is principal. The standard formula is M = P × r / (1 − (1 + r)−n), where P is the amount borrowed, r the monthly rate and n the number of payments.

What is not included

This calculator covers principal and interest only. A real mortgage payment usually also includes property tax, home insurance and sometimes mortgage insurance or association fees, which can add a quarter or more to the monthly figure. Lenders may also charge origination fees that increase the true cost of borrowing.

Ways to pay less interest

  • Choose a shorter term: a 15-year loan has higher monthly payments but far less total interest than a 30-year one.
  • Make occasional extra principal payments, which shorten the loan without changing the required payment.
  • Compare rates from several lenders; a quarter of a percent matters over decades.
