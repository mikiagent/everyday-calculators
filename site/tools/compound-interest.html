Why compounding matters

Compound interest means you earn returns on your previous returns, not only on the money you put in. Over short periods the effect is small, but across decades it dominates: at 7% a year, money doubles roughly every ten years, so a dollar invested at 25 is worth about sixteen dollars at 65 before any further contributions.

Assumptions in this calculator

  • Returns are compounded monthly at a constant rate. Real investment returns vary from year to year.
  • Contributions are added at the end of each month.
  • Taxes, fees and inflation are not included. Inflation of 2% to 3% a year meaningfully reduces what the final balance will buy.

Use the result to compare scenarios, such as starting five years earlier or adding $50 a month, rather than as a prediction of a specific future balance.
