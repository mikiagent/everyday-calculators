What BMI measures

Body mass index divides your weight in kilograms by the square of your height in metres. The World Health Organization groups results as underweight below 18.5, normal weight from 18.5 to 24.9, overweight from 25 to 29.9, and obese at 30 or above.

Limitations

BMI is a screening number, not a diagnosis. It cannot tell muscle from fat, so athletes often score as overweight while carrying little body fat, and it does not account for age, sex, bone density or where fat is distributed. Thresholds may also differ for some ethnic groups and are not meant for children, who use age-adjusted charts. Treat the result as a prompt to talk with a health professional rather than a verdict.
